# Source Policy

## Source hierarchy
### Tier 1 — Primary or authoritative
Peer-reviewed research; government agencies; universities; museums; archives; official scientific institutions; original historical documents; official technical documentation; recognized international organizations.

### Tier 2 — High-quality secondary
Established academic publications; respected journalism; expert institutions; reputable educational organizations.

### Tier 3 — Discovery only by default
Wikipedia, blogs, forums, social media, unspecialized aggregators and general-interest pages. Use to discover terminology or leads, not as sole support for important factual claims.

## Evidence rules
- Prefer sources that directly support the exact claim.
- Numerical, causal, disputed and superlative claims need stronger support than descriptive background facts.
- Check whether newer evidence supersedes older findings.
- For important claims, seek independent confirmation where practical.
- A source's reputation does not rescue a claim it does not actually support.
- Search snippets are discovery aids, not evidence records; preserve the underlying source URL/citation in the source pack.

## Source IDs
Assign `SRC-001`, `SRC-002`, etc. within each topic package. Record title, author/institution, date, URL/DOI, tier, access date, relevant claim IDs and notes.
