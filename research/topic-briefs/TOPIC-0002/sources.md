## SRC-001 — NSF / U.S. Antarctic Program overview

**Institution:** National Science Foundation  
**Tier:** 1 — authoritative institutional source

## SRC-002 — Badgeley et al. 2017
**Title:** An englacial hydrologic system of brine within a cold glacier: Blood Falls, McMurdo Dry Valleys, Antarctica  
**Journal:** Journal of Glaciology 63(239), 387–400  
**DOI:** 10.1017/jog.2017.16  
**Tier:** 1 — peer-reviewed primary research  
**URL:** https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-glaciology/article/an-englacial-hydrologic-system-of-brine-within-a-cold-glacier-blood-falls-mcmurdo-dry-valleys-antarctica/B5C197906AD54619AEA26068AD92989A  
**Supports:** active englacial/subglacial hydrologic system; radio-echo sounding; basal crevassing/pressurization model; 3–6 km upstream source region; elevated salt content + latent heat allowing liquid brine pathways; iron-bearing brine reddens after atmospheric exposure.  
**Limitation:** some mechanism elements are modeled/inferred rather than directly imaged in full 3-D.

## SRC-003 — Sklute et al. 2022
**Title:** A Multi-Technique Analysis of Surface Materials From Blood Falls, Antarctica  
**Journal:** Frontiers in Astronomy and Space Sciences  
**DOI:** 10.3389/fspas.2022.843174  
**Tier:** 1 — peer-reviewed primary research  
**URL:** https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/astronomy-and-space-sciences/articles/10.3389/fspas.2022.843174/full  
**Supports:** modern mineralogical explanation of red-orange material; oxidation of dissolved Fe²⁺; amorphous hydroxylated mixed-valent iron-bearing nanospheres; possible influence of other ions; outflow can emerge clear then redden.  
**Limitation:** analyzes surface materials; does not alone establish the full hydrologic source/trigger.

## SRC-004 — Lyons et al. 2019
**Title:** The Geochemistry of Englacial Brine From Taylor Glacier, Antarctica  
**Journal:** Journal of Geophysical Research: Biogeosciences 124, 633–648  
**DOI:** 10.1029/2018JG004411  
**Tier:** 1 — peer-reviewed primary research / direct clean-entry sample  
**URL:** https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2018JG004411  
**Supports:** direct sampling of hypersaline iron-rich englacial brine; measured brine near −7 °C; marine origin modified by extended rock-water interaction; extensive subglacial aquifer context.  
**Limitation:** one clean-entry sampling campaign and geochemical interpretation; exact age of original seawater remains debated.

## SRC-005 — Mikucki et al. 2009
**Title:** A Contemporary Microbially Maintained Subglacial Ferrous “Ocean”  
**Journal:** Science 324(5925), 397–400  
**DOI:** 10.1126/science.1167350  
**Tier:** 1 — peer-reviewed primary research  
**URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19372431/  
**Supports:** active microbial assemblage in sulfate-rich ancient marine brine; sulfur cycling coupled to iron chemistry; survival without contemporary photosynthesis; anoxic but not sulfidic system.  
**Limitation:** “ocean” is the paper's metaphor/title and must not be converted into a literal cavernous ocean visual.

## SRC-006 — Mikucki et al. 2015
**Title:** Deep groundwater and potential subsurface habitats beneath an Antarctic dry valley  
**Journal:** Nature Communications 6, 6831  
**DOI:** 10.1038/ncomms7831  
**Tier:** 1 — peer-reviewed primary geophysical research  
**URL:** https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms7831  
**Supports:** extensive low-resistivity zones interpreted as briny groundwater beneath Taylor Valley; connection to Blood Falls system; potential subsurface microbial habitat.  
**Limitation:** airborne EM infers conductive liquid/brine; it does not directly photograph groundwater geometry.

## SRC-007 — Carr, Carmichael & Pettit 2022
**Title:** Wintertime Brine Discharge at the Surface of a Cold Polar Glacier and the Unexpected Absence of Associated Seismicity  
**Journal:** Journal of Geophysical Research: Earth Surface 127  
**DOI:** 10.1029/2021JF006325  
**Tier:** 1 — peer-reviewed primary observational research  
**URL:** https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10328233  
**Supports:** episodic hypersaline iron-rich discharge; documented winter release; releases can span weeks to months and occur several times per decade; trigger explicitly unresolved; no strong associated seismic signature in the observed event.  
**Limitation:** one intensively monitored event does not define all release events.

## SRC-008 — Doran et al. 2026
**Title:** Glacier surface lowering and subglacial outflow coincide with Blood Falls discharge in the McMurdo Dry Valleys  
**Journal:** Antarctic Science 38(2), 101–103  
**DOI:** 10.1017/S0954102025100527  
**Tier:** 1 — peer-reviewed primary observation, latest literature checked  
**URL:** https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antarctic-science/article/glacier-surface-lowering-and-subglacial-outflow-coincide-with-blood-falls-discharge-in-the-mcmurdo-dry-valleys/0333F307DC1863B79CA3FCCF162A6373  
**Supports:** a 2018 event coincided with ~15 mm glacier lowering, ~10% velocity slowdown, Blood Falls outflow and a cold-water anomaly in Lake Bonney; provides evidence linking glacier dynamics and discharge.  
**Limitation:** short note / single coincident event; coincidence supports a mechanism but does not prove a universal trigger.
