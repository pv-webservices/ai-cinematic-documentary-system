## Central narrative
A visual mystery becomes a hidden-mechanism explanation: Blood Falls looks biological, but it is an episodic outlet from a cold, hypersaline subglacial system. The red color develops through iron chemistry at the surface; salt and latent heat help liquid brine persist inside the glacier; the system hosts microbial life; and the precise trigger for each release is still not fully settled.

## Viewer question
“How can an Antarctic glacier appear to bleed—and how can liquid water move through ice this cold?”

## Angle type
Visual mystery → hidden mechanism → scientific uncertainty.

## Information order
1. **0–3 s:** Blood Falls visual; establish impossible-looking red stain.
2. **3–9 s:** First reveal: not blood; hypersaline iron-rich brine.
3. **9–18 s:** Color mechanism: brine reaches air; dissolved iron oxidizes; red-orange material forms.
4. **18–29 s:** Hidden system: brine comes from beneath/within Taylor Glacier; do not depict one giant lake.
5. **29–36 s:** Counterintuitive physics: high salinity + latent heat/cryoconcentration keep localized brine pathways liquid; direct sample near −7 °C.
6. **36–42 s:** Optional implication: chemically powered microbial ecosystem under the ice.
7. **42–45 s:** Honest unresolved ending: the exact trigger of individual releases is still being worked out; 2026 data link one event to glacier lowering/slowdown.

## Reveal structure
