# Approved Example Script — VID-0001
Target: ~55–60 seconds. Internal Claim IDs remain in this production copy.

**Hook**
Two thousand years ago, Greek engineers built a gear machine that could mark when eclipses were expected. **[CLM-001, CLM-003]**

**Context**
What survives today is the Antikythera Mechanism: corroded bronze fragments from an ancient shipwreck. **[CLM-001, CLM-006]** Modern surface imaging and X-ray tomography revealed hidden gear trains and inscriptions inside them. **[CLM-004]**

**Escalation**
The gears did not “see” the future. They encoded repeating astronomical cycles. **[CLM-002, CLM-003]** On the back, a spiral dial tracked the Saros cycle — 223 lunar months — a pattern used to forecast eclipse possibilities. **[CLM-003]**

**Reveal**
Turn the mechanism, and its gear ratios carried those cycles onto the dials, turning astronomy into a mechanical display. **[CLM-002, CLM-003]**

**Meaning / implication**
We still do not possess the complete original machine, so some reconstructions remain debated. **[CLM-006, CLM-007]** But the surviving parts are enough to show just how sophisticated Hellenistic astronomical engineering had become. **[CLM-001, CLM-002]**
