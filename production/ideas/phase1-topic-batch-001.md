## C01 — The Lake That Released a Silent Gas Cloud
- **Central question:** How did Lake Nyos release enough carbon dioxide in 1986 to kill people and animals in nearby valleys without a volcanic eruption?
- **Category:** Natural science / disaster
- **Why interesting:** A visually calm lake produced a fast-moving invisible hazard.
- **Curiosity driver:** Disaster mechanism contradicts what viewers expect an “eruption” to look like.
- **Possible hook:** “This lake killed without lava, fire, or an explosion.”
- **Visual opportunities:** crater lake cross-section; dissolved CO2 accumulation; dense gas flowing downslope; degassing pipe.
- **Research challenges:** trigger mechanism was not definitively established; casualty counts vary by source.
- **Uncertainty:** High confidence in CO2 release mechanism; lower confidence in exact trigger.
- **Source availability:** HIGH — USGS multidisciplinary investigation and later limnology literature.
- **Short-form suitability:** HIGH
- **Long-form potential:** HIGH
- **Discovery source:** https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/1987/0097/report.pdf

## C02 — The Roman Concrete Crack That Repairs Itself
- **Central question:** What role might lime clasts and “hot mixing” have played in the durability and self-healing behavior of some Roman concrete?
- **Category:** Engineering / ancient technology
- **Why interesting:** A feature long treated as poor mixing may have been functionally useful.
- **Curiosity driver:** Ancient construction material appears to exploit a crack-sealing mechanism.
- **Possible hook:** “These white chunks may be why some Roman concrete can seal its own cracks.”
- **Visual opportunities:** macro concrete fracture; lime clast cross-section; water entering crack; calcium carbonate precipitation; Roman structure reconstruction.
- **Research challenges:** avoid generalizing one mechanism to all Roman concrete; distinguish experimental replication from archaeological proof.
- **Uncertainty:** Mechanistic evidence is strong for studied samples, but “the secret of all Roman concrete” would overstate it.
- **Source availability:** HIGH — Science Advances paper + MIT/Harvard research context.
- **Short-form suitability:** HIGH
- **Long-form potential:** HIGH
- **Discovery sources:** https://news.mit.edu/2023/roman-concrete-durability-lime-casts-0106 ; https://dspace.mit.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/36797716-7325-4717-8d57-c500d3182afa/content

## C03 — A Day on Venus Lasts Longer Than Its Year
- **Central question:** How can Venus rotate so slowly that one rotation takes longer than one orbit around the Sun?
- **Category:** Space / planetary science
- **Why interesting:** Familiar words “day” and “year” behave counterintuitively on Venus.
- **Curiosity driver:** A true, impossible-sounding comparison.
- **Possible hook:** “On Venus, a day lasts longer than a year.”
- **Visual opportunities:** orbit vs rotation diagram; clock/calendar comparison; retrograde rotation; sunrise direction.
- **Research challenges:** clearly distinguish sidereal day from solar day.
- **Uncertainty:** Low for measured periods; origin of Venus’s unusual rotation requires careful framing.
- **Source availability:** HIGH — NASA planetary data.
- **Short-form suitability:** VERY HIGH
- **Long-form potential:** MEDIUM
- **Discovery source:** https://science.nasa.gov/venus/venus-facts/

## C04 — The Explosion That Flattened a Forest Without an Impact Crater
- **Central question:** What most likely happened over Tunguska in 1908, and why was no conventional impact crater found?
- **Category:** Space / historical mystery
- **Why interesting:** Massive destruction with no classic crater.
- **Curiosity driver:** Evidence points to an atmospheric airburst rather than a ground impact.
- **Possible hook:** “Something flattened about 2,000 square kilometers of Siberian forest—without leaving an impact crater.”
- **Visual opportunities:** atmospheric entry; airburst; shock-wave expansion; radial tree-fall map; expedition reconstruction.
- **Research challenges:** historical measurements are incomplete; exact object properties and breakup remain model-dependent.
- **Uncertainty:** Airburst interpretation is strong; exact size/composition/altitude are estimates.
- **Source availability:** HIGH — NASA historical and technical sources.
- **Short-form suitability:** VERY HIGH
- **Long-form potential:** HIGH
- **Discovery source:** https://www.nasa.gov/history/115-years-ago-the-tunguska-asteroid-impact-event/

## C06 — The Lost Franklin Ships and the Knowledge That Helped Find Them
- **Central question:** How were HMS Erebus and HMS Terror found after more than a century and a half, and what role did Inuit knowledge play?
- **Category:** History / archaeology / exploration
- **Why interesting:** A famous expedition mystery was narrowed and ultimately solved through archaeology, technology, persistence, and Inuit knowledge.
- **Curiosity driver:** The ships were lost for generations, but crucial clues had long existed in local knowledge.
- **Possible hook:** “Two ships vanished in the Arctic for more than 160 years. The search succeeded only after investigators followed evidence that had been there all along.”
- **Visual opportunities:** Arctic map; icebound ships; Inuit route knowledge visualization; sonar reveal; underwater wreck imagery.
- **Research challenges:** respectfully represent Inuit knowledge; avoid claiming wreck discovery fully resolves why every crew member died.
- **Uncertainty:** Ship locations are established; final sequence of crew deaths remains partly unresolved.
- **Source availability:** VERY HIGH — Parks Canada archaeology and Government of Canada records.
- **Short-form suitability:** HIGH
- **Long-form potential:** VERY HIGH
- **Discovery sources:** https://parks.canada.ca/lhn-nhs/nu/epaveswrecks/culture/archeologie-archeology/decouvertes-discoveries/erebus ; https://www.canada.ca/en/parks-canada/news/2017/09/the_wrecks_of_hmserebusandhmsterror.html

## C07 — Doggerland: The Prehistoric Landscape Under the North Sea

- **Category:** Archaeology / geography
- **Why interesting:** A large prehistoric landscape connecting Britain to continental Europe is now underwater.

- **Uncertainty:** Existence/inundation is established; local settlement reconstructions vary in confidence.
- **Source availability:** HIGH — University of Bradford/Europe’s Lost Frontiers + archaeological literature.
- **Short-form suitability:** HIGH
- **Long-form potential:** VERY HIGH
- **Discovery source:** https://www.bradford.ac.uk/staff/vgaffney/

- **Long-form potential:** MEDIUM
- **Discovery source:** https://www.usgs.gov/publications/geology-devils-tower-national-monument-wyoming

## C09 — How Yellowstone Can Explode Without Magma
- **Central question:** How can superheated groundwater cause hydrothermal explosions without a volcanic eruption?
- **Category:** Geology / extreme natural phenomena
- **Why interesting:** “Explosion at Yellowstone” usually implies magma, but hydrothermal explosions have a different mechanism.
- **Curiosity driver:** Water flashing to steam can fragment rock and throw debris hundreds of meters.
- **Possible hook:** “Yellowstone can explode even when magma isn’t erupting.”
- **Visual opportunities:** subsurface hot-water reservoir; pressure/confinement diagram; flash-to-steam expansion; crater/debris reconstruction.
- **Research challenges:** avoid implying imminent volcanic eruption; separate common small hydrothermal events from rare large events.
- **Uncertainty:** Mechanism is well established; exact conditions of individual events can remain under study.
- **Source availability:** VERY HIGH — U.S. National Park Service / Yellowstone Volcano Observatory sources.
- **Short-form suitability:** VERY HIGH
- **Long-form potential:** HIGH
- **Discovery source:** https://www.nps.gov/yell/learn/nature/hydrothermal-systems.htm

## C10 — What a Magnetic Pole Reversal Actually Means
- **Central question:** What happens during a geomagnetic reversal, and what does the geologic record really show?
- **Category:** Earth science / misconception correction
- **Why interesting:** Popular descriptions often treat pole reversals as instant catastrophes.
- **Curiosity driver:** The real process is slower, irregular, and recorded in rocks.
- **Possible hook:** “Earth’s magnetic field has flipped many times—but not the way disaster videos show it.”
- **Visual opportunities:** dynamo diagram; magnetic field lines; lava locking in polarity; seafloor magnetic stripes; reversal timeline.
- **Research challenges:** effects during future reversals are model-dependent; do not equate magnetic and geographic pole movement.
- **Uncertainty:** Historical reversals are established; detailed future consequences are uncertain.
- **Source availability:** VERY HIGH — USGS + geomagnetism literature.
- **Short-form suitability:** HIGH
- **Long-form potential:** VERY HIGH
- **Discovery source:** https://www.usgs.gov/faqs/it-true-earths-magnetic-field-occasionally-reverses-its-polarity

## C11 — Why Pompeii’s Ash Cloud Was Deadly Even Away From the Hottest Zone

- **Why interesting:** The simplistic “everyone was instantly burned” story does not fit every part of the eruption zone.
- **Curiosity driver:** Different locations experienced different lethal mechanisms.
- **Possible hook:** “At Pompeii, heat wasn’t the only thing that made the ash cloud deadly.”
- **Visual opportunities:** Vesuvius eruption column; Pompeii/Herculaneum map; pyroclastic current cross-section; duration/exposure diagram.
- **Research challenges:** competing models of cause of death; human remains require respectful visual treatment.
- **Uncertainty:** Several lethal mechanisms are strongly supported, but exact conditions differed by location and phase.
- **Source availability:** HIGH — peer-reviewed volcanology/archaeology.
- **Short-form suitability:** HIGH
- **Long-form potential:** HIGH
- **Discovery source:** https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-021-84456-7

## C12 — How the Chicxulub Impact Turned Day Into Darkness
- **Central question:** How did impact-generated dust, soot, aerosols, and atmospheric changes disrupt sunlight and ecosystems after Chicxulub?
- **Category:** Earth history / planetary science
- **Why interesting:** The asteroid’s immediate blast was only part of the extinction mechanism.
- **Curiosity driver:** Global ecological collapse depended on what happened in the atmosphere after impact.
- **Possible hook:** “The asteroid strike lasted moments. The darkness afterward helped change life on Earth.”
- **Visual opportunities:** impact sequence; ejecta plume; atmospheric aerosol layers; sunlight reduction; food-web collapse diagram.
- **Research challenges:** separate established impact/extinction relationship from debated relative contributions and duration of individual atmospheric effects.
- **Uncertainty:** Chicxulub’s role in the K–Pg extinction is strongly supported; exact weighting of kill mechanisms is an active research area.
- **Source availability:** VERY HIGH — USGS, Smithsonian, peer-reviewed impact/extinction literature.
- **Short-form suitability:** VERY HIGH
- **Long-form potential:** VERY HIGH
- **Discovery sources:** https://www.usgs.gov/publications/cretaceous-tertiary-extinction-a-lethal-mechanism-involving-anhydrite-target-rocks ; https://airandspace.si.edu/learn/programs/soar-together/planetary-defense
