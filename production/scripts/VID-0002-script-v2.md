## Viewer-facing narration

This Antarctic glacier looks like it’s bleeding. But the liquid coming out of it isn’t red at first.

Blood Falls is hypersaline, iron-rich brine. When it hits the air, dissolved iron oxidizes, creating the red-orange stain.

The brine comes from a hidden system beneath and within Taylor Glacier, moving through pathways in the ice.

So why doesn’t it freeze solid? Salt lowers its freezing point. And as some water freezes, the remaining brine gets saltier and the freezing releases heat. Scientists sampled liquid there at about minus seven degrees Celsius.

But one mystery remains: researchers still don’t fully know what triggers each release.
